Abstract
This paper presents PPO-ALNS, a novel hybrid framework that integrates Proximal
Policy Optimization (PPO) with Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) for
solving the Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (VRPTW). Traditional
heuristic methods struggle with balancing exploration and exploitation, while pure
reinforcement learning approaches face challenges with discrete decision spaces
and complex constraints. Our approach addresses these limitations by formulating
ALNS as a Markov Decision Process, where a PPO agent dynamically guides de-
stroy-repair operator selection, solution acceptance criteria, and search termination
decisions. We design a comprehensive state representation incorporating problem-
specific features and search dynamics, coupled with a four-dimensional action space
for effective search coordination. The compound reward mechanism combines im-
mediate rewards with terminal episode rewards, introducing dynamic penalties and
adaptive early-stopping to optimize solution quality and computational efficiency.
Experiments on VRPTW instances with 20, 50, and 100 customers demonstrate
that PPO-ALNS consistently outperforms traditional ALNS, Ant Colony Optimi-
zation (ACO), and other Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based operator selection
methods, achieving improvements of 11.37–17.41% over baseline approaches. The
performance advantage increases with problem complexity, indicating strong scal-
ability. Ablation studies validate the synergistic effect of simultaneous operator
learning, while sensitivity analysis confirms optimal performance in medium time
window scenarios, highlighting the framework’s effectiveness for complex routing
optimization.

1 Introduction
Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) is one of the important combinatorial optimization
problems where an optimal route to visit customers is constructed. This problem
plays a significant role in various logistics and transportation systems, making it a hot
topic in both operations research and computer science (Kim et al. 2015). The practi-
cal applications of VRP are expanding rapidly, including parcel delivery (Enthoven
et al. 2020), urban waste collection (Akbarpour et al. 2021), and ride-sharing services
(Martins et al. 2021).
The basic VRP model assumes that vehicles can serve each demand point at any
time without specific time requirements. However, in many real-world problems,
there are often time window constraints (Time Windows, TW), meaning that custom-
ers require service to be completed within a specific time period. This additional time
constraint significantly increases the complexity of the problem, forming the Vehicle
Routing Problem with Time Windows (VRPTW), which was proposed by Solomon
(1987). In VRPTW, each vehicle not only needs to plan its route reasonably but also
must ensure that it arrives at the customer within the specified time window, avoiding
penalties or additional costs associated with early or late service. Therefore, VRPTW
is considered a more complex and challenging combinatorial optimization problem
than the classic VRP (Kosolsombat and Ratanavilisagul 2023).
In recent years, various approaches have been proposed by academia and industry
to solve the VRPTW, including exact algorithms (Toth and Vigo 2002; Archetti et al.
2011; Costa et al. 2019; Baldacci et al. 2012), heuristic algorithms (Solomon 1987;
Mei et al. 2017), and metaheuristic algorithms (Ursani et al. 2011; Zhong and Pan
2007; Ho and Haugland 2004; Dixit et al. 2019; Gambardella et al. 1999). Although
exact algorithms can theoretically find the optimal solution, their computational time
increases exponentially with the problem size, making them impractical for large
instances. Heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms strike a balance between compu-
tational efficiency and solution quality, but they still face limitations when dealing
with complex VRPTW, especially in terms of balancing solution quality and compu-
tational time for large-scale problems with multi-dimensional constraints (Li et al.
2021). Recently, optimization methods based on deep learning and reinforcement
learning (RL) have emerged, showing promising application prospects (Kosolsom-
bat and Ratanavilisagul 2023; Falkner and Schmidt-Thieme 2020; Deng et al. 2024;
Wang et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024). However, these methods still face challenges in
handling discrete decision spaces and complex time window constraints. Addition-
ally, traditional RL algorithms often lack global search capabilities and are prone to
getting stuck in local optima.
To overcome these limitations, hybrid algorithms combining RL and heuristic
methods have been explored (Fernandes et al. 2009; Zhao et al. 2021; Gao et al.
2020). These hybrid methods can be roughly divided into two types, one is to use
heuristics to improve the solutions generated by end-to-end RL methods, and the
other is to use RL methods as a support tool to solve the internal subproblems of
heuristics (Bogyrbayeva et al. 2024). These hybrid approaches aim to leverage the
strengths of both methods: the global search capability of RL and the local refinement
ability of heuristic algorithms. However, existing hybrid algorithms still face issues

such as insufficient exploration of the solution space, a tendency to get trapped in
local optima, and slow performance when solving large-scale problems. Addition-

Generally, the main contributions of this research are summarized as follows:
● We propose a novel hybrid framework (PPO-ALNS) that integrates Proximal
Policy Optimization with Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search. Our framework
enables the PPO agent to dynamically guide the selection of destroy-repair opera-
tors, solution acceptance criteria, and algorithm termination timing based on real-
time problem states, achieving synergistic collaboration between policy learning
and heuristic search.
● We design a comprehensive state-action space specifically tailored for VRPTW,
incorporating multidimensional features such as time window constraint tight-
ness, customer spatial distribution patterns, historical operator performance sta-
tistics, and search trajectory characteristics. Our four-dimensional action space
facilitates intelligent coordination of exploration-exploitation trade-offs during
ALNS iterations, enhancing adaptive decision-making capabilities.
● We develop an innovative compound reward mechanism that combines immedi-
ate step-wise rewards with terminal episode rewards. Our design introduces dy-
namic penalties for rejecting superior solutions and accepting inferior ones, along
with an adaptive early-stopping reward mechanism that optimizes the balance
between solution quality and computational efficiency. This approach effectively
prevents premature convergence while preserving search diversity throughout the
optimization process.
In addition to addressing complex logistics challenges, the proposed PPO-ALNS
framework is also highly relevant for cross-domain emergency planning and schedul-
ing. In scenarios such as natural disasters, public health emergencies, or other urgent
events, timely and efficient decision-making is critical. The ability of our method to
rapidly re-optimize routes and resource allocation in response to dynamically chang-
ing conditions underscores its potential as a valuable tool for emergency manage-
ment. By facilitating real-time data integration and adaptive decision-making, our
approach can contribute to reducing response times and enhancing coordination in
high-stakes operational environments.

2 Related work
In this section, we first review the existing methods for solving VRPTW problems,
including precision algorithms, heuristics, RL methods, and hybrid methods, and
point out their limitations. Then, we discuss the related research in the field of ALNS,
especially the method of operator selection. Finally, we explain how to combine RL
methods with ALNS.
2.1 Algorithms for VRPTW
VRPTW is an extension of the classical VRP, where customers must be served within
specific time windows. Due to its significance in real-world applications such as trans-
portation, delivery services, and supply chain management, VRPTW has become a
key research topic in logistics and operations research (Liu et al. 2023). Compared
to VRP, VRPTW incorporates time window constraints, requiring each vehicle to
arrive at the customer location within a specified time frame, which complicates the
optimization process.
Exact algorithms including Branch-and-Bound (Toth and Vigo 2002) and Branch-
and-Price (Costa et al. 2019) guarantee optimal solutions for small instances but
become computationally intractable for larger problems due to exponential search
space growth. Consequently, heuristic and metaheuristic approaches dominate practi-
cal applications.
Early heuristics such as Solomon (1987)’s insertion method and Clarke & Wright
savings algorithm (Republic et al. 2016) provide computational efficiency but often
yield suboptimal solutions due to local decision rules. Metaheuristic algorithms
including Genetic Algorithms (GA) (Ursani et al. 2011), Simulated Annealing (SA)
(Zhong and Pan 2007), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) (Dorigo et al. 2006; Gam-
bardella et al. 1999), and Tabu Search (TS) (Ho and Haugland 2004) achieve better
global optimization. Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) (Shaw 1998) and its adap-
tive variant Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) (Pisinger and Ropke
2007) have emerged as leading metaheuristics, dynamically adjusting search strate-
gies for enhanced flexibility.
However, these methods require extensive parameter tuning and struggle with
exploration-exploitation balance (Zhang et al. 2022). Recent machine learning
advances have introduced RL techniques to routing problems. Nazari et al. (2018)
demonstrated Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)’s competitiveness with tra-
ditional heuristics, while Kool et al. (2019) extended this using attention models
and REINFORCE for large-scale instances. Wu et al. (2022) developed self-atten-
tion-based improvement heuristics for the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) and
Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), significantly outperforming existing
methods.
RL applications to VRPTW include Falkner and Schmidt-Thieme (2020)’s Joint
Attention Model for Parallel Route-Construction (JAMPR), which constructs mul-
tiple routes concurrently. While RL offers adaptive optimization without problem-
specific rules, challenges remain in convergence speed, sample complexity, and
discrete action space handling. PPO (Schulman et al. 2017) addresses some limi-

tations through constrained policy updates and improved stability, but alone may
struggle with VRPTW’s vast solution space and hard constraints.
Hybrid algorithms combining RL with metaheuristics (Fernandes et al. 2009; Zhao
et al. 2021; Gao et al. 2020) leverage complementary strengths: RL learns efficient
strategies through dynamic decision-making, while metaheuristics explore solution
spaces on a large scale.
Cappart et al. (2021) integrated RL with constraint programming to solve VRPTW
using dynamic programming approaches. Zhao et al. (2021) combined AC algorithms
with local search for large-scale VRP optimization. Chen et al. (2020) developed
Variable Neighborhood Search with RL for periodic VRPTW. Qin et al. (2021) pro-

excellent performance on large-scale problems. Gupta et al. (2022) introduced DRL
with attention-based encoder-decoder frameworks for capacitated vehicle routing
problem with time windows (CVRPTW), balancing solution quality and computa-
tional efficiency.
Hottung and Tierney (2020) proposed Neural Large Neighborhood Search com-
bining deep learning with RL for CVRP and split delivery vehicle routing problem
(SDVRP). Lopes Silva et al. (2019) developed multi-agent framework AMAM for
VRPTW using RL and meta-heuristic cooperation. Da Costa et al. (2021) applied
DRL to learn 2-opt strategies for TSP and VRP variants.
Despite progress, existing hybrid methods exhibit limited solution space explo-
ration, local optima entrapment, and computational inefficiency for large-scale
problems.
2.2 Adaptive large neighborhood search
Since its introduction by Ropke and Pisinger (2006), ALNS has become a core meta-
heuristic framework for solving VRP and its variants. Its core mechanism dynami-
cally adjusts the selection probabilities of destroy and repair operators to balance
exploration and exploitation during the search process.
Classical ALNS employs history-based operator selection, categorizing perfor-
mance into three groups: global optimum improvements (

(2021) shows adaptive layers achieve marginal 0.14% improvement, recommended
only for specialized contexts due to limited effectiveness and increased complexity.
Destroy and repair operator design critically governs ALNS adaptability and opti-
mization efficacy. Common destroy strategies include randomness-driven, cost-based
elimination, and sequence-oriented destruction, while repair operations employ
greedy heuristics, regret-value prioritization, or stochastic logic. Empirical analysis
reveals sequence-based destroy operators and regret-enhanced repair strategies dem-
onstrate superior performance (Voigt 2025). Hybrid configurations combining con-
ventional destroy sets with sequence-aware mechanisms achieve enhanced outcomes
while maintaining solution diversity.
Solution acceptance criteria critically impact ALNS performance. Dynamic cri-
teria permitting conditional inferior solution acceptance–simulated annealing (SA),

Record-to-Record Travel (RRT), and Threshold Acceptance (TA)–generally outper-

optima escape effectiveness. Santini et al. (2018) demonstrate linear RRT achieves
robust performance across vehicle routing, minimum spanning tree, and quadratic
assignment problems, while random walk and hill climbing underperform due to
insufficient exploration-exploitation balance. Dynamic criteria integration enhances
ALNS-based heuristics’ robustness and solution quality.
Termination criteria selection balances computational efficiency and solution
quality, considering resource constraints and performance objectives (Windras Mara
et al. 2022). Common strategies include fixed iteration limits, dynamic stagnation
thresholds, runtime restrictions, and temperature thresholds. Fixed iterations offer
simplicity, while dynamic termination enables adaptive control based on solution
stagnation patterns. Termination criteria must be aligned with specific problem char-
acteristics and practical resource limitations to achieve optimal trade-offs between
computational efficiency and solution quality.
2.3 Combine RL with ALNS
Recent advancements in integrating RL with ALNS have marked a pivotal break-
through in combinatorial optimization, where RL’s dynamic decision-making capa-
bilities enhance ALNS’s heuristic search process to improve adaptability and solution
efficiency. Reijnen et al. (2024) proposed the DR-ALNS framework, pioneering DRL
for online dynamic configuration of ALNS, including operator selection, destruc-
tion intensity adjustment, and SA criteria control. Its design demonstrates superior
performance over traditional ALNS and Bayesian optimization methods with strong
generalization capabilities. Kallestad et al. (2023) developed the DRLH framework,
replacing ALNS’s conventional roulette-wheel mechanism with DRL-based deci-
sion-making. Their approach robustly handles large operator sets across multiple
combinatorial optimization problems while outperforming standard ALNS. Johnn
et al. (2024) introduced Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for state representation in
their GRLOS method, managing complex combinations of 28 destroy/7 repair opera-
tors across five routing problem types. The graph-structured RL policy maintains
efficiency even in 1,000-node instances. Wang et al. (2025) designed hybrid state
features for the Capacitated Electric Vehicle Routing Problem (CEVRP), integrating
charging strategy selection into DRL-driven ALNS, achieving 7 out of 8 optimal
solutions in medium-scale instances. These studies advance the integration of RL
and ALNS by overcoming limitations of traditional weight-based mechanisms, with
notable progress in state representation, operator generalization, and complex con-
straint handling.
3 Methodology
Our proposed PPO-ALNS framework establishes intelligent control over combina-
torial optimization processes through DRL. The framework formulates the ALNS
algorithm as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) environment, where a PPO-driven

agent executes four-dimensional decisions at each iteration: (1) Dynamically selects
destroy operators from a predefined library based on current solution characteristics;
(2) Adaptively matches repair operator combinations according to post-destruction
solution structures; (3) Evaluates solution quality and search status to determine
acceptance of new solutions as subsequent search baselines; (4) Monitors conver-

4 Experiment
4.1 Experiment details
4.1.1 Data generation
We generate VRPTW instances of sizes 20, 50, and 100 for model training and test-
ing. Customer node coordinates are randomly generated from a uniform distribution
over 
[0, 1] × [0, 1]
. The depot coordinates are determined as the geometric center of
all customer nodes. We assume sufficient vehicle availability (set equal to the num-
ber of customer nodes) with a fixed vehicle capacity of 64. Customer demands are
randomly generated from the interval [1, 16], service times from [0.05, 0.1], and time
windows from [0.1, 0.3]. The maximum travel time is set to 1. All pairwise Euclid-
ean distances between customer nodes are normalized through division by a scaling
factor of 50.
4.1.2 Operator sselection
Our PPO-ALNS framework integrates five destroy operators and three repair opera-
tors to dynamically adjust the search process.
Destroy Operators: Random Destroy, String Destroy, Route Destroy, Worst-Cost
Destroy, and Sequence Destroy.
Repair Operators: Greedy Repair, Criticality-Based Repair, and Regret-k Repair.
Detailed descriptions of these operators are provided in Appendix B. The PPO
agent learns to adaptively select operators based on real-time solution states, balanc-
ing exploration and exploitation during the search.
4.1.3 Initial solution
Two independent initialization strategies are employed to generate initial solutions
for comparative experiments:
● Greedy Initialization: A feasible solution is constructed by iteratively insert-
ing the nearest customer node that satisfies both time window and capacity con-
straints. This method prioritizes rapid solution generation but may converge to
local optima.
● Clarke-Wright (CW) Savings Initialization: The solution is generated through

4.1.4 Baseline algorithms
We compare our proposed PPO-ALNS with the following baseline algorithms:
Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search ALNS is a metaheuristic algorithm that
iteratively improves solutions by dynamically selecting and applying destroy and
repair operators. In the context of the VRPTW, ALNS removes a subset of custom-
ers from the current solution using adaptive destruction heuristics and reinserts them
using repair operators. The selection of operators is guided by a roulette wheel mech-
anism that prioritizes historically high-performing operators, enabling the algorithm
to balance exploration and exploitation. ALNS excels in handling complex VRPTW
constraints, such as time windows and vehicle capacity, by leveraging its adaptive
nature to escape local optima and systematically refine solutions.
Ant Colony Optimization ACO is a swarm intelligence-based metaheuristic
inspired by the foraging behavior of ants. For VRPTW, artificial ants construct fea-
sible routes by probabilistically selecting the next customer based on pheromone
trails and heuristic information (e.g., proximity or time window urgency). Phero-
mone levels on edges are updated to reflect solution quality, reinforcing paths asso-
ciated with low-cost solutions. The algorithm inherently balances exploration (via
probabilistic selection) and exploitation (via pheromone-guided intensification),
making it effective for combinatorial optimization problems like VRPTW. ACO’s
ability to handle dynamic constraints and generate diverse solutions through parallel
agent-based search has established it as a robust baseline for route optimization in
time-constrained environments.
4.1.5 Parameter settings
For all experiments, we employ 32 parallel environments for asynchronous training
with a total of 1,600,000 training steps. The initial learning rate is set to 0.001 using
the Adam optimizer, with a batch size of 128. The discount factor 
γ 
is configured as
0.99, and the maximum number of iterations is limited to 100.
The PPO algorithm utilizes a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) architecture for both
policy and value networks, which share identical feature extractors. These networks
consist of three fully-connected layers with [512, 256, 128] hidden units and ReLU
activation functions, excluding the final output layers. The policy network’s final
linear layer projects to the action space dimension, while the value network’s final
layer produces a scalar output.
During testing, the ALNS baseline algorithm’s iteration count is synchronized
with PPO-ALNS. The adaptive mechanism employs roulette wheel scores [25, 5,
1, 0] corresponding to four quality levels of accepted solutions, accompanied by a
decay coefficient of 0.9 for score adjustment.

5 Conclusion

icy Optimization with Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search for VRPTW. By formu-
lating ALNS as a MDP with a four-dimensional action space and compound reward
mechanism, our approach enables intelligent coordination of destroy-repair operator
selection, solution acceptance, and termination decisions.
Experimental results demonstrate PPO-ALNS’s consistent superiority over tra-
ditional methods, achieving 11.37–17.41% improvements across problem scales of
20-100 customers. The performance advantage increases with problem complexity,
indicating strong scalability. Ablation studies validate the synergistic effect of simul-
taneous operator learning, while sensitivity analysis confirms optimal performance in
medium time window scenarios.

Appendix B Operator details
This section provides detailed descriptions of the destroy and repair operators used in
our PPO-ALNS framework.
Appendix B.1 Destroy operators
● Random Destroy: Eliminates a randomly selected subset of customer nodes
from the current solution.
● String Destroy: Removes continuous route segments containing randomly se-
lected anchor customers.
● Route Destroy: Selects complete routes for elimination using inverse-length
probability weighting, where shorter routes receive higher removal likelihood.
This mechanism promotes diverse exploration of routing configurations.
● Worst-Cost Destroy: Prioritizes high-impact node removal through cost-benefit
analysis. Incorporates a randomization factor to balance greedy elimination and
stochastic exploration (Liu et al. 2024).
● Sequence Destroy: Disrupts route continuity by eliminating random contiguous
subsequences from concatenated route representations (Li et al. 2016).
Appendix B.2 Repair operators
● Greedy Repair: Inserts unassigned customers into the best feasible route posi-
tion that minimizes travel cost increase. If no feasible insertion exists, creates a
new route for the customer.
● Criticality-Based Repair: Prioritizes customers by a composite criticality met-
ric (demand magnitude, time window tightness, and depot proximity), inserting
high-priority customers first to enhance solution quality.
● Regret-k Repair: Employs a dynamic regret mechanism (with adaptive k based
on problem scale) to select insertions that maximize the cost difference between
the best and subsequent insertion options, balancing immediate and future inser-
tion costs (Qu and Bard 2012).
