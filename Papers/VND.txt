A B S T R A C T
This paper addresses the bi-objective optimization problem of scheduling jobs on multiple identical parallel
machines to minimize both makespan and total energy consumption. The problem can be denoted as
𝑃 𝑚, 𝑇 𝑂𝑈 ∣∣ 𝐶
𝑚𝑎𝑥
, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶. A Pipe Variable Neighborhood Descent approach with Dynamic Programming,
employing an Epsilon Constraint method and incorporating an Epsilon Oscillation technique that consists
of alternating between ascending and descending ways to vary the epsilon value. This technique improves
the pareto front by exploring new search space. A comparison is provided between the proposed Epsilon
Oscillation Algorithm (EOA) and the state-of-the-art solving techniques. The experimental results as well as the
statistical tests show the significant superiority of the EOA, motivating its adaptation to solve other bi-objective
scheduling problems.
1. Introduction
Decision makers must change and evolve their companies and op-
erations to be able to survive on the market in the long term. In fact,
today’s companies are experiencing high levels of change in terms of
both internal and external pressures, described by PESTELE factors.
The Global Risks Report (2022), that reveals the top risks facing the
world, is dominated by environmental risks, mainly the climate change
that encompasses global warming. Regarding the high importance of
the sustainability, the Sustainable Development Report (2024) reviews
progress made each year on the Sustainable Development Goals (17
SDGs) since their adoption by the 193 UN Member States in 2015.
To help researchers and institutions track and demonstrate progress
towards the targets of the SDGs, the Elsevier has generated a set of
Scopus queries related to each of the SDGs (Elsevier BV, 2019). Ac-
cording to UNIDO (2024), the industry is the responsible for more than
one third of global primary energy consumption and energy-related
carbon dioxide emissions. These issues have increased the interest in
actions aiming at improving energy efficiency in industry (Despeisse
et al., 2012). Furthermore, improving energy efficiency in industry
is one of the most cost-effective measures to help supply-constrained
developing and emerging countries meet their increasing energy de-
mand and loosen the link between economic growth and environmental
degradation, such as climate change. To pursue energy efficiency,

2. Literature review
Energy-efficient scheduling under TOU prices has been widely in-
vestigated in various scheduling environments: single machine schedul-
ing (Che et al., 2016; Fang et al., 2016), parallel machine (Che et al.,
2017; Zhou et al., 2018), flow shop (Badri et al., 2021), job shop (Kur-
niawan et al., 2020), and flexible Jobshop (Gong et al., 2019; da Jiang
and Wang, 2020). For more studies on energy-efficient scheduling,
please refer to the review article of Li and Wang (2022).
Ronco (2022) proposed a systematic classification of the literature
of TOU scheduling distinguishing the problems that are easily repre-
sentable with the three-field notation and solvable to optimality with a
polynomial or a pseudo-polynomial time algorithm and others NP-hard,
from those that contain particular characteristics such as technological
features or unusual objective functions or have applicative problems
inspired by a practical case study. Ronco (2022) highlighted that the
𝑃 𝑚 ∣∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶 studied in this paper is NP-hard and belongs to the
first class. We mention in what below some relevant works on the ex-
tensive field of multi-objective energy-efficient scheduling, minimizing
Cmax and TEC under TOU prices in general before focusing on our
specific problem 𝑃 𝑚, 𝑇 𝑂𝑈 ∣∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶.
Regarding the single-machine TOU scheduling problem, Cheng et al.
(2014) provided a bi-objective MILP formulation for the NP-hard prob-
lem 1 ∣ 𝑏𝑎𝑡𝑐ℎ(𝑏) ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶. The authors developed an algorithm
based on the epsilon-constraint method for multi-objective optimization
that is based on solving a sequence of single-objective formulations
of the original problems. Cheng et al. (2016) and Wu et al. (2021)
dealt with the same problem and proposed a faster optimal epsilon-
constraint algorithm and two new fast epsilon-constraint-based con-
structive heuristics, respectively. Rubaiee and Yildirim (2019) dealt
with the denoted 1 ∣ 𝑝𝑟𝑚𝑝 ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶, and proposed a mixed-
integer multiobjective mathematical programming model and several
new holistic ant colony optimization algorithms. Zhou et al. (2018)
considered a batch scheduling problem with release dates denoted 1 ∣
𝑏𝑎𝑡𝑐ℎ(𝑏), 𝑟𝑗 ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶, and provided a mathematical formulation and
a hybrid multi-objective meta-heuristic algorithm.
Wang et al. (2020a) provided a Discrete optimization model for
permutation flow shop under time-of-use electricity tariffs denoted
𝐹 𝑚 ∣ 𝑝𝑟𝑚𝑢 ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶. The optimization model uses central moment
method modeling to ensure the continuity of job-processing and the
uniqueness of processing time points. Wang et al. (2020b), got Inspired
by a real-world glass-ceramics production process, investigated a bi-
criteria energy-efficient two-stage hybrid flow shop scheduling problem
denoted 𝐻𝐹 𝑆, 𝑇 𝑂𝑈 ∣ 𝐵 ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶, and proposed a mixed integer
programming (MIP), based on which an augmented epsilon-constraint
method is adopted to obtain the exact Pareto front. They also proposed
a problem-tailored constructive heuristic method with local search
strategy, a bi-objective tabu search algorithm and a bi-objective ant
colony optimization.
Li et al. (2018) provided a new mixed-integer linear programming
to deal with the 𝐽 𝑚 ∣ 𝑏𝑎𝑡𝑐ℎ(𝑏) ∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶. da Jiang and Wang (2020)
consider a flexible job shop denoted 𝐹 𝐽 𝑐 ∣∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶 and proposed
a mixed integer programming model and a hybrid multi-objective
evolutionary algorithm based on decomposition.
Wu and Che (2019) studied an energy-efficient bi-objective unre-
lated parallel machine scheduling problem to minimize both makespan
and total energy consumption. They addressed an integrated energy-
efficient scheduling problem of production and shipping. The prob-
lem is modeled as a mixed-integer quadratic program, and consider-
ing its complexity, a memetic algorithm is proposed to incorporate a
knowledge-driven local search strategy with a balance between explo-
ration and exploitation. Pei et al. (2021) tackled the unrelated parallel
machines problem with multiple objectives 𝑅𝑚 ∣∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶. The au-
thors first linearly combined the two objectives and provided a MINLP
formulation for the resulting single objective problem; and exploited a
relaxation of the problem to develop an approximate algorithm.
To the best of our knowledge, Wang et al. (2018) is the first
study on bi-objective green identical parallel machine scheduling un-
der TOU prices, derived from a real-world automobile stamping die
manufacturing. They extended the single-objective model in Che et al.
(2017) to a bi-objective mixed integer programming for minimiz-
ing TEC and makespan simultaneously, by introducing two variables
and three constraints related with the completion time of jobs. An
augmented epsilon-constraint method is adapted to obtain the exact
Pareto front. The problem is NP-hard since it can be reduced to 𝑃 𝑚 ∣∣
𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥 (Garey and Johnson, 1978). Owing that, Wang et al. (2018)
proposed two methods: a constructive heuristic method with a local
search strategy and an application of the NSGA-II algorithm to obtain
good approximate Pareto fronts in a reasonable computation time.
Subsequently, Anghinolfi et al. (2021) addressed the same problem and
developed an ad-hoc heuristic method. The first part of the method,
called Split-Greedy heuristic, consists in an improved and refined
version of the constructive heuristic proposed in Wang et al. (2018) and
the second part, called Exchange Search, is a novel local search proce-
dure aimed at improving the quality of the Pareto optimal solutions.
To the best of our knowledge, these two papers contain the best and
most recent solving techniques for 𝑃 𝑚, 𝑇 𝑂𝑈 ∣∣ 𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶 problem.
Although, this problem requires further investigation as the efficiency
of the existing solving techniques is limited and need to be improved
to solve real-world instances. Therefore, the same problem 𝑃 𝑚, 𝑇 𝑂𝑈 ∣∣
𝐶𝑚𝑎𝑥, 𝑇 𝐸𝐶 will be considered in this study, and an Epsilon Oscillation
Algorithm (EOA) combining an epsilon constraint exploration strategy
with a Pipe Variable Neighborhood Descent (Pipe-VND) and Dynamic
Programming (DP) is provided and compared with Anghinolfi et al.
(2021), and Wang et al. (2018).

4. Proposed algorithm: Epsilon Oscillation Algorithm (EOA)
The proposed approach is a Pipe-VND approach with DP employ-
ing an epsilon constraint and incorporating an Epsilon Oscillation
technique. This technique alternates between ascending and descend-
ing methods to adjust the epsilon value systematically. This dynamic
adjustment enables the generation of diverse Pareto fronts at each
iteration. The Pipe-VND is the local search used to generate different
sequences of jobs on machines, and the DP used to generate the optimal
schedule respecting the sequence on each machine. We used the 𝜀-
constrain to deal with two objectives TEC and makespan 𝐶

that we present here, and consists of alternating the ascending way
and descending ways 𝑛 times instead of using only one way 2𝑛 times.
The Epsilon Oscillating Strategy is an advanced optimization technique
used primarily in multi-objective optimization to effectively manage
the trade-offs between competing objectives. This strategy involves
periodically adjusting the emphasis on each objective by modifying
epsilon 𝜖 values, which represent small allowable deviations for the
objectives. Initially, one objective may be prioritized by assigning it
a stricter epsilon value. Over time, these values are systematically
oscillated, shifting the algorithm’s focus among objectives either cycli-
cally or adaptively based on the solution’s performance. This dynamic
adjustment allows the exploration of different regions of the solution
space, aiding in avoiding local optima and fostering a diverse set
of Pareto-optimal solutions. The process continues until a predefined
stopping criterion is met, such as a certain number of iterations or a
convergence threshold.
Through the experimentation, the Epsilon Oscillating offers a better
exploration of the pareto front, Nevertheless, in addition to the oscil-
lating epsilon, the use of a perturbation procedure remains necessary
starting from the second iteration of the Epsilon Oscillation to guaran-
tee generating different pareto front at each iteration. The perturbation
procedure considered in our algorithm is presented in the next section
(see Table 3).
4.4. Perturbation procedure
The solution provided at each iteration of the Epsilon Oscillating
procedure is subjected to a perturbation phase using some random
moves. The perturbation is used as a diversification mechanism to
prevent that the Epsilon oscillation does not generate the same pareto
front at each iteration. During the perturbation phase, one machine and
one position are randomly selected and the job located at the selected
position is inserted in all possible positions among all other machines.
This process is repeated a certain number of times (fixed to three in our
case) to guarantee the diversification (Fig. 3).

6. Conclusions and perspectives
This study investigated a bi-objective optimization problem of
scheduling jobs on multiple identical parallel machines to minimize
both makespan and total energy consumption. The provided approach
denoted EOA combines a Pipe-VND with DP, and employs an Epsilon
Constraint method that incorporates an Epsilon Oscillation technique
that alternates between ascending and descending variations of the
epsilon value. Extensive experiments are conducted on a benchmark
instances and performance measures are employed to assess the quality
of the proposed approach against state-of-the-art algorithms. The re-
sults demonstrate that our approach yields high-quality solutions with
the enhancement by the Oscillation technique that serves to further
explore the search space, and improves the Pareto front approximation.
However, it is worth noting that when solving large instances, our
method encounters challenges in terms of CPU times due to the use
of the DP to optimally solve each sequence generated by the Pipe-
VND. Looking ahead, our method can be further explored and applied
to solve other scheduling problems, such as bi-objective optimization
problem of green flowshop scheduling. As perspective, we expect better
solving the looping problem by addressing a Tabu list instead of using
the perturbation procedure.
